THE SPECIAL CASE

The arc property

DEFINITION

Far(q) = points at distance ≥ r from q. P has the r-arc property if every Far(q) is one contiguous run along the hull.

Unimodal point sets: walking along the hull from q, the distance to q only rises, then only falls. They have the arc property for every r.

Examples: points on or near a circle, and rounded or jittered regular polygons. In our tests, every hull of random points also had the arc property at its optimal radius.

Circle: Far(q) is one arc (blue). Holds.

Thin lens: Far(q) splits into two runs (blue and orange). Fails.

[Name 3] · Part 3

Define Far(q) as the points at distance at least r from q. The arc property says that, for every q, these points are consecutive along the hull. On a circle this is obvious: distance grows as you walk away from q and shrinks as you come back. On a thin lens it fails: from a point on the lower chain, the far points are the right part of the lens and also the left tip, with near points in between. Unimodal sets, where every distance profile rises then falls, have the arc property at every radius. These are exactly the thin shapes where Part 2’s counterexamples live.
